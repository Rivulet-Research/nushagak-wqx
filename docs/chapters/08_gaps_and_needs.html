Data Gaps and Monitoring Priorities

Summary of Baseline Assessment

The existing water quality data for the Nushagak watershed provide a partial baseline. Data are dominated by USGS streamflow and periodic chemistry, supplemented by research project sampling.

Critical Gaps

1. Spatial Coverage

  • Wood River (19030304) has limited established monitoring infrastructure; within the Lower Nushagak sub-basin (19030303), the Nuyakuk River tributary specifically is undersampled relative to mainstem gauges in that same HUC8 (see Spatial Coverage chapter)
  • Tributary streams in all sub-basins are underrepresented
  • No monitoring in headwater reaches where geochemical and thermal conditions differ markedly

2. Temporal Frequency

  • Most existing data are episodic grab samples rather than continuous or systematic time series
  • No baseline for seasonal or interannual variation in key parameters
  • High-flow events are rarely sampled (logistics, accessibility)

3. Parameter Breadth

Parameter Class Status Priority
Temperature (continuous) Few loggers High
Dissolved oxygen Limited observations High
Nutrients (N, P) Sparse High
Trace metals Very limited Medium
Biological (fish, invertebrates) Dispersed across agencies High
Acidification potential Essentially absent Medium

4. Data Management and Sharing

  • Limited standardization of field methods and data entry
  • No real-time or near-real-time data sharing platform among agencies and communities

Notes on Monitoring Priorities

This section intentionally does not prescribe site counts, parameters, or timelines

Deciding where new monitoring is most valuable requires judgment about tribal priorities, logistics, funding, and risk. This section lays out the structured process by which those choices could be made, and the open questions that need answers before the plan can be finalized.

How monitoring priorities should be decided

The EPA’s Data Quality Objectives (DQO) process is the standard framework for translating monitoring goals into a defensible sampling design: it specifies the problem, the decision to be made, the data needed to support that decision, and acceptable levels of uncertainty, before site counts or frequencies are chosen (EPA 2006). Any site counts, parameter lists, or sampling frequencies proposed for the Nushagak effort should be the output of this process, run with the three participating tribes, rather than a number proposed in this document.

Open questions requiring community input

  • Which sub-basins or reaches do the Native Village of Ekwok, New Koliganek Village Council, and New Stuyahok Village Council consider highest priority, based on local observation and traditional knowledge, not just the spatial coverage gaps identified above?
  • What specific water quality concerns (e.g., observed changes in fish health, turbidity after development activity, temperature trends) have communities already raised that should drive site placement?
  • What funding, staffing, and equipment constraints realistically bound the number of sites and the monitoring timeline?
  • Are there existing tribal environmental program plans, natural resources priorities, or prior monitoring proposals that should set the baseline design rather than a new one drafted here?
  • What does the RFP itself specify, in terms of required deliverables, timeline, or budget, that constrains feasible scope?
